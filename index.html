01 • Why it matters02 / 11

A few words can carry a lot of care.

A condolence message does not remove grief. It simply tells someone: you are not alone.

Express

Say that you are genuinely sorry for the loss.

Remember

Honor the person with one respectful detail.

Support

Offer warmth, prayer or quiet encouragement.

03 • Do’s & Don’ts04 / 11

Kind words are also careful words.

Do

  • Be sincere and specific.
  • Keep the tone gentle and respectful.
  • Mention one positive quality or memory.
  • Offer support without demanding a response.
  • Check names, relationships and spelling.

Don’t

  • Use clichés such as “everything happens for a reason.”
  • Make the message about your own grief.
  • Ask intrusive questions about the death.
  • Give advice the family did not ask for.
  • Use humor, casual slang or excessive detail.

Rule of thumb: comfort, don’t explain.

04 • Mediums of delivery05 / 11

The medium changes the tone — not the kindness.

✦

Handwritten card

Best when you want a tangible, personal gesture. Keep handwriting clear, the message concise and the design restrained.

Best for: family, close friends, formal condolences.

@

Email

Useful when distance or timing matters. Use a clear subject line, short paragraphs and a respectful sign-off.

Best for: colleagues, teachers, distant contacts.

•••

Social message

Public spaces need extra restraint. Keep it brief and avoid private details; offer to speak privately if appropriate.

Best for: a short public acknowledgment.

HEARTFELT CONDOLENCES!
D.O.B. 25 July 1983
D.O.D. 30 July 2026
Late Mr. Nirmal Purja (Nimsdai)

We are deeply saddened to share the sudden passing away of Mr. Nirmal Purja, a world-renowned mountaineer, former Gurkha and Special Boat Service soldier, and a source of immense pride for Nepal. He devoted his life to pushing the boundaries of human endurance and inspiring people around the world to believe that nothing is impossible.

Mr. Purja was known for his extraordinary courage, determination and humility. He set a world record by climbing all fourteen of the world's 8,000-metre peaks in just six months and six days in 2019, a feat chronicled in the Netflix documentary "14 Peaks: Nothing Is Impossible." He also led the historic first winter ascent of K2 with an all-Nepali team and championed greater recognition for Nepali climbers.

We are deeply grieved by the loss of such a distinguished and inspiring person. We extend our heartfelt condolences to all who mourn his passing. His contribution, character and service will be remembered with gratitude. May his departed soul rest in eternal peace.

Nepal Mountaineering Association

07 • Ready-to-use examples09 / 11

Keep the focus on the deceased.

Each example follows the same three-paragraph idea: life, character, contribution — then condolence and prayer.

Friend’s parent

Warm + personal

“We were deeply saddened by the passing of Mr. Arun Rai, a gentle and generous teacher. He was admired for his patience, his love of books and the encouragement he gave to young people. His contribution to education will be remembered with gratitude. We extend our heartfelt condolences to all who mourn him. May his departed soul rest in peace.”

Colleague

Formal + dignified

“It was with great sadness that we learned of the passing of Ms. Mira Kandel, a dedicated professional and respected mentor. Her careful work, integrity and willingness to guide others made a lasting contribution to our institution. We extend our sincere condolences to those who mourn her loss. May her departed soul rest in eternal peace.”

Distant relative

Respectful + concise

“We were deeply saddened to hear of the passing of Mr. Hari Bahadur Joshi, a respected member of our extended family and community. He was remembered for his humility, kindness and strong sense of service. We offer our heartfelt condolences to all who mourn him and pray that his departed soul may rest in peace.”

08 • Final checklist10 / 11

Before you submit

Pause. Read it once more.

✓
Is the first sentence sympathetic?

No long introduction.

✓
Did you honor the person?

One sincere detail is enough.

✓
Did you offer support?

Comfort without pressure.

✓
Is the ending respectful?

Sign your name and, when required, address.
